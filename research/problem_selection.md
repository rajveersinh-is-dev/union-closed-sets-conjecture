# Problem Selection

## Selected target

**Frankl's Union-Closed Sets Conjecture (1979).**

Statement: Every finite union-closed family of sets $\mathcal F\neq\{\varnothing\}$ contains an element belonging to at least half of the member-sets.

Formal: $\mathcal F\subseteq\mathcal P([n])$ union-closed, $\mathcal F\neq\{\varnothing\}$
$\Longrightarrow$ $\exists x\in[n]$ such that $|\{A\in\mathcal F:x\in A\}|\ge |\mathcal F|/2$.

## Open-status verification (performed before selection)

The conjecture was checked against multiple independent sources:

1. **Wikipedia, "Union-closed sets conjecture"** — describes it as an open problem posed by Péter Frankl in 1979; cites recent work (Tian 2021, Liu 2023 arXiv:2306.08824, Lu–Raz 2024 arXiv:2405.10639).
2. **Nikola Veselinov, "On the current state of the Union-closed sets conjecture" (Aug 13, 2025)** — states the conjecture is open; best constant $c\approx 0.3823455$ (arXiv:2212.12500v2).
3. **Henning, "The journey of the union-closed sets conjecture" (survey, uni-ulm.de)** — confirms open; verified for families on $\le 12$ elements (Zivković–Vučković); "natural weakening" (constant fraction) still open.
4. **Gil Kalai's blog (Nov 17, 2022)** — Gilmer's first constant lower bound; describes the conjecture as open and persistent.

Classification: **Confirmed open.** No solution was found in any source checked. The most recent relevant literature (2022–2025) treats it as open.

## Candidate pool evaluated and rejected

The following were investigated and rejected as the main target:

| Candidate | Reason rejected |
|-----------|-----------------|
| Traversal by Prime Sum (prime-sum graph Hamiltonicity) | Solved for sufficiently large $n$ (arXiv:2609.19114, 2026); remaining small cases are computational. Not open in the needed form. |
| Griggs–Yeh L(2,1)-labeling conjecture ($\lambda\le\Delta^2$) | Solved by Havet–Reed–Sereni (SODA 2008; Hal 2012). |
| Ben Green Problem 1 (large sum-free sets) | Solved by Bedert (2025, Quanta/Annals-line). |
| Davenport constant formula $D(C_n^r)=1+r(n-1)$ | Disproved by Wang (2026, "Disproof of a Conjectured Upper Bound for the Davenport Constant"). |
| Erdős–Gyárfás, Caccetta–Häggkvist, reconstruction, Hadwiger, etc. | Famous but no plausible entry point; difficulty is a major breakthrough, not a tractable gap. |

## Why Frankl's conjecture was selected

* **Confirmed open** across current, independent sources.
* **Elementary statement** — no heavy machinery required to understand or to verify small cases computationally (exact integer enumeration).
* **Precise, quantitative gap:** conjecture $=1/2$ vs. best bound $c\approx 0.382$. A new bound, a new proof technique, or a proof for a new class of families would be a genuine partial result (Category B or C).
* **Mathematical insight is the decisive factor**, not computation scale. Small enumeration is used only to explore structure and test lemmas.
* **Plausible intermediate targets:** prove for a new class of families; improve the constant $c$; prove a structural lemma; clarify the "natural weakening."

## Research targets (per the mission framework)

* **Target A (full result):** Prove Frankl's conjecture in general. (Acknowledged as very hard; the ambition, not the expectation.)
* **Target B (intermediate):** Prove the conjecture for a new infinite class of union-closed families, or improve the general lower-bound constant above $c\approx 0.382$.
* **Target C (weaker/accessible):** Prove the conjecture for a restricted class (e.g., families generated by a small number of sets, or families with bounded maximum set size beyond the known cases).
* **Target D (obstruction):** A necessary condition or impossibility result ruling out a specific approach.
* **Target E (independent structural):** A structural theorem about union-closed families that is of interest even if the full conjecture remains open.
